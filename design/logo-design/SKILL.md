# Logo Design

A logo rarely fails because it is badly drawn. It fails because it says the wrong thing, looks like every other mark in its category, or breaks down where it actually lives: in an avatar circle, a mobile header, on a label, in motion. This skill keeps the work tied to meaning, ownability and real use.

## Modes

- **concept**: from brief to concept directions.
- **review**: score an existing logo or a set of concepts.
- **handover**: check or prepare the files and guidance for the client.
- No mode given: pick the one the request implies and say which in one line.

## The 12 principles

### Meaning (before drawing)

| # | Principle | Rule | Typical mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Context first** | Start from the real places the mark will live, chosen for this brand: the smallest important one, a header, the main product or surface, dark backgrounds, motion. Put the plain name into them before drawing | Designing on an empty artboard, then dropping the result into a mockup and hoping |
| 2 | **Requirements, not adjectives** | Turn brief words like "modern", "premium", "trustworthy" into requirements you can judge: what the audience must understand, what sets the brand apart, where people meet it | Two people read "premium" and both are right, so nothing can be judged |
| 3 | **Concept from substance** | Look for ideas in what the business does, how it does it, and what people get from it. Explore lettering as well as symbols. Add a symbol only when the brand needs one | The first association of the industry, in six variants |
| 4 | **Voice alignment** | Know how the brand speaks before shaping it: words it would never say, words it overuses, one sentence it would introduce itself with. The mark matches that temperature | A loud brand with a timid mark, a calm brand with aggressive angles |
| 5 | **One idea in one sentence** | Every concept can be explained in one sentence. If it can't, it is too complicated. Simplify once it works | A mark that combines four ideas and expresses none |
| 6 | **Ownable in its category** | Survey the category: which shapes, type styles and colors crowd it. Decide on purpose whether to stand apart or to fit in. Then claim a space no one holds | A mark that could swap places with a competitor's and nobody would notice |

### Proof (while choosing)

| # | Principle | Rule | Typical mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | **Compare in real conditions** | Judge concepts side by side under equal conditions: same background, same size, then in the environment that matters most. Test the smallest important placement early | Choosing the concept that looks best large on white |
| 8 | **Test beyond your own opinion** | Put it next to competitors at equal size. Show it briefly to people, hide it, ask them to find it among others. Show it without explanation and collect the words people use for how it feels; compare them with the requirements | Approval by the designer's taste alone |

### System (after choosing)

| # | Principle | Rule | Typical mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | **Versions with a purpose** | Each version exists because a real placement needs it, checked at its real size (a full wordmark where there is room, a compact mark where the name can't be read) | Variants made to fill a brand guide |
| 10 | **Motion follows form and character** | Movement starts from the direction the mark already has and matches the brand character (restrained brands move slowly and rest, energetic ones move with drive) | A bounce or spin that contradicts the brand |
| 11 | **Clean construction** | In outline view, every point earns its place: no redundant points on straight lines, no curves fighting their handles, each curve doing one job | Hidden point clutter that renders and scales slightly off |
| 12 | **Handover answers real questions** | The client can tell which file goes on the website, which in the avatar, what goes to print and how, without asking. Clear file names, an example of each version in use, a note where a physical sample is needed before approval | A folder of files named final_v3 |

A principle that does not apply (no motion planned, no handover yet) is **n/a** and does not count.

## Concept: steps

1. Collect the brief, the brand's voice and the places the mark will live. Missing information becomes a question, never an assumption presented as fact.
2. Write the requirements (principle 2) and the voice notes (principle 4).
3. Survey the category and note what crowds it (principle 6). Use real research where available; do not invent competitors.
4. Develop three or more directions from different starting points (what, how, what people get; lettering and symbol). For each: the one sentence, why it fits the requirements, where it stands in the category.
5. Show each direction in the most important real placements at real size, including the smallest one. Drawn concepts (e.g. SVG) are fine; keep them simple and say they are concepts.
6. Recommend one direction with reasons tied to the requirements, and list which tests (principle 8) to run with real people.

## Review: steps

1. Look at the mark large, small and in outline view if vectors are available, plus its real placements if known.
2. Answer each principle with Yes, No or n/a and concrete evidence.
3. Output the report.

### Report format (keep it short)

```
# 7 / 10

| # | Principle | Result | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Ownable in its category | No | Coffee bean in a circle, same as most local roasters | Start from how they roast, not from the bean |
| 10 | Motion | n/a | No motion use planned | |
...
```

At most one closing sentence: the biggest lever.

## Handover: steps

List the real use cases (website, avatar, documents, print, product, dark backgrounds, motion), assign one file and version to each, name files after their use, add one example image per version in use, state color values and minimum sizes, and flag anything that needs a physical proof.

## Output

- Keep replies short, one point per reply.
- Provide created files as clickable links.
- No invented research, test results or competitor names; mark them as to be done.
